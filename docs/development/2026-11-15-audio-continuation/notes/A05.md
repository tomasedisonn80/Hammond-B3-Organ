## Whole-plan findings from the complete range

1. **Driver and cabinet boundaries, pp. 337–348:** nominal 16-ohm part identity
   is not a measured impedance curve. Electrical, mechanical, acoustic and
   thermal states have separate units and coupled ownership. Reciprocity and
   power balance provide numerical tests, not Leslie parameters. Page 339
   identifies the source discrepancy between copper coefficients 0.039 and
   0.00393 per degree C; the actual driver's material remains unknown. Static
   response, moving propagation, rotor trajectory, stereo/mono and physical
   uncertainty need separate acceptance. Never double-apply a driver transfer
   already contained in a voltage-to-microphone IR.
2. **Acquisition and provenance, pp. 349–360:** retain actual specimen/revision,
   load, port polarity, calibration, clocks, permissions and immutable raw bytes.
   Historical Table V is measured at preamp G–G, not a generator pickup. Unknown
   calibration is not unity or zero. Exact source keys 1–91, finite strict JSON,
   actual asset hashes and explicit effect ownership precede compilation.
   Referenced companion schemas/results are source claims; reading them here
   does not mean these absent assets or experiments exist in this repository.
3. **Identification and regression, pp. 361–372:** fit upstream boundaries first;
   output-only gains can remain structurally ambiguous. Freeze tolerances and
   holdouts before fitting, preserve failures and distinguish inconclusive from
   pass. Disable leakage for ideal linear-source identity tests, then test it
   independently (p. 369). Preserve phase, original event ordering, zero-frame
   behavior and equivalence across callback partitions. A single combined
   documentary/synthetic/physical “1:1” score is explicitly rejected (p. 372).
4. **Listening and performance, pp. 373–384:** similarity, discrimination,
   equivalence and preference are distinct questions. No listening trials are
   executed in these chapters. UI success cannot certify audio. Callback work
   must be bounded and allocation/file/lock free; immutable preparation and
   delayed reclamation remain outside processing. Proposed arithmetic counts
   and timing budgets are not measured CPU results.
5. **Core, adapters and compatibility, pp. 385–408:** one shared prepared graph
   should serve offline, standalone and VST3 clients. Preserve explicit units,
   waveform boundary and effect ownership; a loaded terminal recording cannot
   receive embedded filtering/coupling again. Compile unresolved inputs only by
   refusing them or selecting a clearly named surrogate. All semantic parameter
   IDs, four retained banks, state migrations and half-open event timing remain
   independent of the view. The atlas's 62 proposed parameters are not authority
   to replace the current 47 implemented identities. Packaging, signing, actual
   host/device trials and physical fidelity need their own executed evidence.
6. **UI, pp. 409–420:** walnut/charcoal/brass and sculpted controls are the art
   direction; generated image text and counts are not engineering authority.
   Geometry, materials, semantic text and state are separate layers. Four
   percussion switches retain enable, level, decay and harmonic, with upper-B
   eligibility displayed from applied routing (pp. 410, 418). Store the switch
   position even when inactive. No decorative charge meter or invented measured
   decay time. Show edited and sounding banks separately, keep all real keys,
   and preserve note/gesture ownership through focus, resize and workspace moves.

These findings support an additive gain-only leakage experiment with explicit
normalized/pre-contact boundary and synthetic provenance. They do not authorize
measured coupling values, downstream idle noise, a second source clock, automatic
live graph replacement or double-counting coupling already included in a future
loaded-terminal waveform. An explicit offline option should preserve the nominal
default and label the alternate source path in its receipt.
