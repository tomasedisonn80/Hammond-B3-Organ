## M7 requirements supported by these pages

### 1. Define two acoustic inputs and their meaning

The responsibility map separates organ electronics, Leslie electronics,
crossover/drivers, independent rotating paths, and microphones/room (p. 4).
The vintage-cabinet discussion distinguishes stationary drivers from horn/drum
motion (p. 56). A first acoustic module should accept separately identified horn
and drum excitations at a declared synthetic boundary. It must state whether
their units are normalized amplitude, pressure, volume velocity, or another
quantity and which transformations are already included. A normalized fixture
must not acquire a physical-volts or pascals claim through a name change.

The atlas's nominal 800 Hz dividing-network label and 15-inch bass-driver label
on p. 56 do **not** specify a complete transfer function, driver impedance,
crossover topology, or geometrical acoustic-source model. Do not invent a
particular filter order and call it the factory crossover. M6 remains a separate
owner and future integration dependency. The boundary should support replacing
synthetic excitations with independently qualified electrical/driver outputs.

### 2. Give horn and drum separate persistent motion

Page 57 calls for independent states in radians and radians per second, with
explicit command semantics. Page 59 requires separately timestamped speed
traces and a photographed positive-rotation convention. Page 60 requires that a
horn speed change leave the drum unchanged unless the command explicitly
targets both. Leslie motion must also remain distinct from scanner phase and
its controls (p. 51).

For a proposed dynamics model, name the model and its assumptions. Do not infer
measured inertia, friction, belt tension, response time, stop angle or rotation
direction from attractive default behavior. Timestamped commands and the audio
sample clock own the state; UI animation consumes a snapshot. The general
persistent-state and independent-display rules are established on pp. 34, 40,
46 and 48. Any stop/brake behavior needs a declared historical applicability or
an explicit software-extension label (pp. 16, 55).

### 3. Make geometry and time conventions inspectable

Page 59 proposes a cabinet-floor-center origin and requires a recorded front
axis and positive-rotation convention. Page 57 requires SI internal units and
preserves exact unit conversions independently of measurement uncertainty.
Choose one documented frame and reference phase, name rotor centers, effective
acoustic positions and microphone coordinates, and expose those quantities in
offline traces. The illustrative model may use proposed geometry, but no value
becomes measured merely because it is in a profile.

Page 29 specifies the dimensional relation `tau = r / c`. It labels
`f_observed approximately = f_source * (1 - d(tau)/dt)` an educational
approximation under a stated slow source-time variation assumption. This is
insufficient to choose a full moving-source solver by transcription alone.
The later M7 reviewers must settle source-time versus receiver-time evaluation,
delay causality, interpolation, bounds and solver residuals before acceptance.
No independent arbitrary pitch LFO should duplicate the Doppler effect already
created by the changing path.

### 4. Assign every acoustic effect exactly one owner

Page 29 separates changing path length from frequency-dependent directivity;
p. 56 separates electrical loading from radiation and propagation while warning
that a one-way chain may lose real coupling. Pages 67-70, 73-78 and 79-84 repeat
the source-boundary rule for pickup filtering and leakage. Apply that rule to
the cabinet: identify which component owns propagation, attenuation, directivity,
cabinet filtering, microphone response and room response, including which are
absent from the first synthetic model. A recording or filter that already
contains an effect must not receive it again.

The baseline 122 does not automatically contain the optional reverb branch
(pp. 56, 60). A dry diagnostic path must remain observable and independently
testable; fitting cabinet or room parameters cannot conceal an upstream error
(pp. 12, 73, 84).

### 5. Keep variant and image provenance out of hidden defaults

The atlas permits common acoustic software for 122/147 only where applicable,
while maintaining separate electrical profiles (p. 14). It distinguishes tall
122/147 from short 142/145, 21H on/off arrangements, and modern servo-driven
122H/142H (p. 55). Do not label a generic trajectory calibrated to all of them.
Cabinet shape, amplifier identity and motor dynamics are independent references
that must be checked for compatibility (p. 57).

The inspected horn/lower-rotor photos on pp. 57-58 support component illustration,
not exact serial or dimensional evidence. Retouched P020 is specifically excluded
from geometry recovery (pp. 55, 58). Cabinet envelope dimensions on p. 55 cannot
determine rotor radius, throat length, driver location or internal source offset.
