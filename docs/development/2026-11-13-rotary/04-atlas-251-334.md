## Electrical and acoustic input ownership

The 122 audio input uses full differential voltage `vd = va - vb`; per-leg
voltages are `vc + vd/2` and `vc - vd/2` (p. 255). The 147 source-load selector
is an amplifier-input branch, not its speaker crossover (pp. 261, 272). These
are future M6 boundaries and must not be silently implemented by normalizing a
single dry audio channel.

The atlas's crossover nodes provide the following source-reported contract
(pp. 271-274): `L2 = 3.2 mH` and the treble load share A-H; `C16A = 7.8 uF`
connects H-B; `L3 = 5.2 mH` connects A-W; `C16B = 12.5 uF` and the woofer
load share W-B. Thus `VT = VA - VH`, not a grounded H output; bass is `VW - VB`.
The nominal 800 Hz label is not an exact acoustic crossover or permission to
substitute Linkwitz-Riley filters. This review has not independently traced
the underlying service drawings, and the rotary scope does not implement this
electrical network.

For this session, input is best declared at each **effective radiation point**
(p. 301). Choose normalized research units or calibrated acoustic units
explicitly; do not imply pascals when no calibration exists. Driver response,
horn internal transit, crossover, load coupling, cabinet reflection, microphone
response and room response are either excluded or owned by a separately named
stage. A future measured complete response must not receive those effects a
second time (pp. 278, 305, 325, 331).

The horn's dummy side must not receive a duplicate waveform (pp. 277-278).
The bass point is an effective outlet approximation, not an orbiting woofer
(pp. 283-285). A drum radius cannot inherit the horn radius. Multiple acoustic
maxima per revolution do not establish multiple driven horns or a higher
mechanical speed (pp. 282, 287, 311).

## Motion equations and stable implementation requirements

Each rotor needs its own nonnegative speed magnitude `omega`, direction
`sigma` in `{ -1, +1 }`, angle/turn representation, initial state, Slow/Fast
targets and upward/downward time constants. Angles are radians, speeds rad/s,
and times seconds (pp. 290, 292, 319-320). The relation `omega = 2*pi*RPM/60`
converts **acoustic rotor** RPM; it must not consume a motor-shaft reading.

For a constant-command segment starting at time `t0`, put `h = t - t0` and
select positive `tau` from the sign of `omega_target - omega_initial`:

```text
omega(h) = omega_target + (omega_initial - omega_target) * exp(-h/tau)

delta_theta / sigma = omega_target*h
  + (omega_initial - omega_target)*tau*(1 - exp(-h/tau))
```

At a command boundary, evaluate the old segment exactly at that boundary,
preserve its speed and angle, and create the new anchor. A Fast-to-Slow command
can still require acceleration if current speed remains below Slow; switch
labels alone cannot select `tau` (p. 320). Repeated unchanged commands must not
restart motion (p. 319). A zero-duration evaluation leaves state unchanged.

The direct angle expression loses precision near start from rest and small
`h/tau`. The atlas prescribes the remainder form (p. 320):

```text
z = h/tau
g(z) = z + expm1(-z) = z^2/2 - z^3/6 + z^4/24 - ...
delta_theta / sigma = omega_initial*h
  + (omega_target - omega_initial)*tau*g(z)
```

Use the series in the small-argument region rather than subtracting nearly
equal terms. Evaluate decaying residual speed directly for long decay toward
zero; check both start-from-rest cancellation and residual underflow against
an independent high-precision reference. The exact branch threshold and error
budget are implementation decisions to freeze before grading, not manufacturer
specifications. The atlas's printed 80-digit and semigroup results on p. 324
belong to absent original companion files, not this new code.

The 5-8 second bass Tremolo-to-Chorale service adjustment has no stated settling
threshold and is neither a universal `tau` nor a horn timing specification
(pp. 293, 319). The reduced model's 10-90 percent time is `ln(9)*tau` (p. 292).
Modern 122H servo defaults do not identify vintage motor constants (p. 289).
Slow/Fast is the baseline; exponential decay to zero is not a finite-time
physical brake, and stop/coast/accessory behavior needs another profile
(pp. 294, 323).

## Retarded propagation and causal interpolation

The proposed scene frame is right-handed: +x to the right when facing the
cabinet front, +y toward its rear, +z upward; azimuth is `atan2(y,x)` and the
front has azimuth `-pi/2` (p. 314). This is a project convention. Each rotor
has a separately identified axis, angle-zero marker and sign.

For a direct circular effective mouth at emission time `e`:

```text
q(e) = axis_origin + [radius*cos(theta(e)), radius*sin(theta(e)), 0]
v(e) = sigma*omega(e)*[-radius*sin(theta(e)), radius*cos(theta(e)), 0]
r(e) = norm(microphone - q(e))
u(e) = (microphone - q(e))/r(e)
F(e) = e + r(e)/c - observation_time
F'(e) = 1 - dot(u(e),v(e))/c
received_frequency/source_frequency = 1/F'(e)
```

These are the atlas's moving-source relation, not an independent pitch LFO
(pp. 279, 301-302). A fixed observation position needs a unique subsonic root:
positive path distance and positive derivative throughout the admitted domain.
Reject a microphone intersecting the modeled circular path; do not hide zero
distance behind an arbitrary pressure clamp.

With known path bounds, bracket `e` between `t - r_max/c` and `t - r_min/c`.
Build an independent bracketed offline reference first, then use a finite
safeguarded runtime iteration budget and certify residuals over the full
admitted envelope (p. 304). The atlas's four-Newton-step synthetic example
does not establish a universal budget. An unconstrained convergence loop or
silent residual failure cannot be accepted in the audio path.

Historical motion is essential. A root just before a speed command must use
the earlier segment even when reception occurs after the command. All receivers
share one physical horn history and one drum history, but query different
emission times (pp. 301, 304, 322). Define negative-time audio/trajectory history
at preparation explicitly; this review recommends making that startup policy
part of the contract rather than silently extrapolating the newest segment.
Store enough history for maximum physical delay plus interpolation and fixed
algorithmic support. Rate changes require a new clock epoch and deliberate
history rebuilding/transition; preserving old sample indices is invalid.

For waveform sample position `Fs*e = k + mu`, `k = floor(Fs*e)` and
`0 <= mu < 1`, linear interpolation is `(1-mu)*x[k] + mu*x[k+1]`.
The cubic prototype uses nodes `k-1, k, k+1, k+2` and Lagrange weights
`w_j(mu) = product_(l != j) (mu-l)/(j-l)` (p. 303). Integer endpoints, negative
indices, final support sample and ring wrap need explicit tests. Centered cubic
support requires additional fixed processing latency if the minimum physical
delay does not provide enough future samples (p. 304). Report that latency
separately from modeled acoustic propagation.

Static cubic accuracy is not a production passband or anti-alias guarantee.
The local maximum resampling ratio suggests an input passband below
`Fs/(2*ratio_max)` with transition margin, but time variation also generates
sidebands (p. 303). Compare both static fractional-position errors and dynamic
spectra against a demonstrably converged reference, preserving failures.

## Receiver, directivity and future filter contracts

If `u` points from source to receiver and `a` is the microphone forward axis,
microphone incidence is `dot(a,-u)` (p. 314). Using `+u` reverses front/rear.
One path's far-field pressure approximation is `reference_distance/r`, not
its square; measured near-field behavior needs its own model (pp. 279, 308).
An ideal omni/unity research response must retain that name and its excluded
effects rather than present itself as a calibrated microphone or Leslie
directivity profile.

Sum signed pressure coherently before output mixing. Complete left/right
receiver swaps should swap outputs; identical coincident channels should
equal their normalized `0.5*(L+R)` fold-down. A spaced scene may have genuine
negative correlation or cancellation; correcting it by independent rotor
clocks would model a different system (pp. 313-318). A rigid transform of the
whole ideal scene should preserve relative geometry.

Future angle-dependent filtering must declare its two-time operator
(pp. 325-330). For fixed causal basis filters `g_r` and periodic weights `b_r`:

```text
Model A: y[n] = sum_r b_r(theta[n]) * (g_r*x)[n]
Model B: y[n] = sum_r (g_r * (b_r(theta)*x))[n]
```

These agree at fixed angle but differ during motion and old impulse tails.
The mouth-response stream can use a named quasi-static Model A, evaluated
with the same emission history; a directional excitation entering a fixed
room can motivate Model B. They are not interchangeable optimizations.
Keep all filter histories running, retain phase and absolute timing, and avoid
per-angle loudness normalization. Profile replacement needs warmed state and
a defined transition; equal-power fading identical correlated outputs adds
approximately 3.0103 dB at its midpoint (p. 327).
