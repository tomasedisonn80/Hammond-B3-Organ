## M7 data and effect ownership

The new module needs a named, proposed fixture when specimen inputs are missing.
A schema-valid all-unknown profile cannot execute until every required algorithm
input is resolved or an explicitly identified surrogate is selected (pp. 355-358,
386). Unknown gain is not unity, unknown distance is not zero, and an arbitrary
speed/radius is not a factory setting.

For each horn/drum excitation and microphone output, retain the quantity, units,
polarity, coordinate convention, source boundary and effects already included.
The separate drivers remain stationary while acoustic structures rotate
(p. 337). Driver response, horn transit, propagation, cabinet, microphone and room
must have one owner. In particular, a terminal-voltage-to-microphone IR already
contains a driver transfer; placing another full driver before it duplicates that
response (p. 340). The same issue applies to propagation embedded in an IR.

Room code is not required to qualify the proposed motion/path foundation. When it
is added, preserve direct-path ownership and complete tails. A deliberately
duplicated coherent direct path is a useful error fixture: the atlas derives a
6.0206 dB amplitude error, while differently delayed paths require a complex
interference comparison rather than that scalar rule (pp. 335-336).

Profile and evidence tooling should reject duplicate JSON keys, nonfinite values,
unresolved required references and unsupported versions; verify actual file
bytes/size/header metadata; and preserve original documents and derivation
recipes. Sorted JSON keys alone do not establish JCS canonicalization (p. 358).
Acquisition grouping and rights travel through derivatives. A local row check
does not establish full ancestry or global split independence (p. 359).

## Physical validation and calibration remain separate work

Before a specimen-specific claim, acquire the installed cabinet/revision, drivers,
divider, panels, motor assemblies, source/load boundary, surveyed microphone
positions, temperature/warmup and permissions (pp. 343-354). Two datasets are
needed for different questions:

1. Stationary angle/pose/level captures establish complex response with absolute
   gain and arrival time. Freeze geometry or document residual motion. A sweep
   through continuous rotation is not automatically a stationary IR (p. 345).
2. Dynamic captures retain input, microphones, horn angle, drum angle and command
   timing on a shared or calibrated timebase. Test steady speeds, both ramps,
   interrupted reversals and multiple initial angle pairs (p. 346).

First replay independently measured trajectories into the acoustic renderer;
then substitute predicted motor dynamics. This isolates propagation/radiation
error from motion error. Acoustic phase spikes near cancellation nulls cannot
identify mechanical acceleration. Retain exclusions and angular/time coverage;
a once-per-revolution marker does not resolve arbitrary within-cycle motion
without an uncertainty-bearing interpolation (p. 346).

Estimate measurement resolution with a pilot and freeze budgets before holdout
evaluation. Retain common gain, clock and position covariance; repeated biased
recordings do not remove common uncertainty. Use calibrated complex transfer,
arrival timing, trajectory, sidebands, stereo coherence and mono sum. An
unresolvable budget is **inconclusive**, not an easier certification target
(pp. 347, 354, 363-366). Do not independently realign every revolution, normalize
each registration or add final EQ to erase the error under test.

Split complete acquisition groups before optimization, retain parent lineage and
lock a fresh final set whenever final failures guide another revision. Numerical
termination and a visually good fit cannot establish parameter identifiability.
Preserve effective parameter interpretations when the actual internal quantities
are not observable (pp. 359, 361-366).

Listening is a later, separately designed experiment. Preserve a calibrated
absolute-level comparison alongside any declared fixed playback gain. Similarity,
ABX discrimination, equivalence and preference are different endpoints; none is
a percentage of physical authenticity. Freeze margin, population, material,
analysis, blinding and stopping rules, then retain condition-level failures and
uncertainty (pp. 373-378). No such trial was performed by this assignment.
