## Required invariants for continuing development

### Shared sources and musical routing

The 91 electrical source identities remain separate from shaft IDs, pickup IDs, terminal locations, MIDI pitches, playing-key numbers and zero-based array indexes (pp. 91–97). One shared source value is produced for a sample instant and consumed by every route selecting it (pp. 100, 103). Note on/off, preset selection, silent periods and manual bypass cannot restart these sources (pp. 88–90, 100, 105–108). A zero-frame call must not advance time or touch absent audio buffers (p. 106).

The manual map uses playing keys 1–61, the offset vector `[0,19,12,24,31,36,40,43,48]`, the 16-foot low fold for keys 1–12 and **repeated** octave subtraction while a source exceeds 91 (pp. 98–99). In the standard manual profile sources 1–12 are unreachable; that constraint must not be copied to pedals (pp. 99, 102). Foldback collisions share phase but retain separate contact states and electrical branches. At top key 61, drawbars 2/5/8 select tone 80 and drawbars 4/6/9 select tone 85 (p. 100).

Nominal drawbar mixing uses physical 0=mute and 8=full. The proposed amplitude law is `g(0)=0`, `g(p)=10^(3*(p-8)/20)` for p=1…8 (pp. 109–111). Two identical coherent routes double voltage, approximately +6.0206 dB; summing powers or normalizing by held-key count changes the required model (p. 111). Nominal gains are not production transformer measurements. Loaded networks must preserve independent branches until their electrical interactions are solved (pp. 110–114).

### Contact audio: the immediately useful M3 requirement

The ideal fixed-load reference is `u(t)=sum_j a_j*c_j(t)*s_w(j)(t)`. A connection change yields `Δu_j=a_j*Δc_j*s_w(j)(t_edge)` (p. 116). At a source zero crossing the value jump can be zero while the derivative jump remains nonzero. A renderer that corrects only amplitude steps therefore has an incomplete anti-aliasing argument. Compare the complete switched waveform, including simultaneous/colliding-source events and tails, against an independently resolved reference.

Each key owns nine histories influenced by a common gesture, with separately defined make/break behavior and per-contact thresholds (p. 117). Ordinary MIDI initiates a named synthetic trajectory; velocity can affect its transient without becoming steady-state volume. Store absolute frame plus fractional time, define stable equality ordering and keep stochastic identity independent of buffer size or GUI timing (p. 117). A single 1967 C-3 contact study cannot provide universal B-3 thresholds, release timings or bare-contact resistance (pp. 115, 117, 119).

Resolve source phase and switching **before** reducing bandwidth. Oversampling an already aliased base-rate click cannot remove that error (p. 118). Prepared contact/event/tail/filter storage must be bounded, with explicit overflow behavior; filters and pending corrections persist across callbacks and note release (pp. 118–120). Publish actual delay and distinguish patch data from a snapshot of running phases and pending events.

Page 120 proposes normalized RMS error `RMS(candidate-reference)/RMS(reference) <= 1e-4` for agreed **nonzero-reference** synthetic fixtures after gain/latency conventions are fixed. It labels this as an engineering proposal, not a Hammond specification or demonstrated pass. It supplies no silence normalization rule, finite resource budget, full-window tail contract or admission for the current repository. These must be frozen before implementing/grading the next audio module. Existing independent contracts and their original failures cannot be replaced by this broad atlas target.

### Presets, percussion and event ownership

Cancel is a momentary cradle action that releases preset connectivity while preserving playing-key ownership and source phases; it does not stay visibly latched and cannot cancel the other manual or pedals (p. 121). Four drawbar banks persist independently. Selecting A#/B changes connectivity instead of copying positions (pp. 121, 126, 153). The 162-digit chapel table is documentary and requires its own source review; it is not proof every specimen uses those registrations (p. 122).

Upper B controls percussion eligibility. Its keying route is the **eighth harmonic, 1-foot**, not the eighth left-to-right drawbar (pp. 124, 127–128). The detector derives from physical contact/routing state even if the audible drawbar is silent. One shared control history persists when another note joins a chord. A same-time off/on has zero recharge duration; duplicate owners must not manufacture new envelopes (pp. 129–132).

The RC surrogate uses exact exponential evolution for its stated constant-parameter first-order equation, not for the entire AO-28 circuit. Page 135's nominal products are 1.551 s, about 0.375 s and 0.03432 s. They are calculated electrical starting points, not measured audio decay or recharge constants. The voltage-to-gain mapping, fast-mode bias, normal/soft gain and upper-path compensation require distinct treatment and evidence (pp. 133–138). Percussion reuses the shared harmonic sources and must not double-add the sustained return (p. 136).

Input adapters retain origin, bus/channel/identity, original destination and press sequence. Mapping changes affect future presses; old releases use retained destinations (pp. 151–152). Multiple owners hold one physical key until the final owner releases it. Raw MIDI and VST3 zero-velocity behavior cannot be conflated. The atlas proposes discrete routes before notes at an equal offset, preserved note order and stable contact order; this is a declared software policy, not sub-sample hardware evidence (p. 153). CC120, CC121, CC123 and global Panic have separate scopes and must preserve unrelated ownership/source phase as contracted (p. 156).

### Pedals, expression and electrical boundaries

Pedals consume the same shared generator field through eight contact columns and four buses. The exceptional third-contact column repeats p+12 for pedals 1–7, is disconnected for 8–12, then uses p+19 for 13–25 (pp. 139–140). All 200 positions comprise 195 connected entries and five nulls. Duplicate source IDs are separate paths; null is not tone zero. Complex intrinsic harmonics follow their own wheel's phase, whereas borrowed source harmonics use the separate source's gearing and phase (pp. 141–144). Reactor inductance/loss, contact resistances, loaded transfer and measured low-wheel spectra remain unknown.

Expression belongs at its defined common preamp boundary. Controller endpoint/inversion calibration, physical travel, loaded electrical response and downstream monitor master gain are distinct (pp. 145–150). A frozen-coefficient capacitive surrogate omits the `v*dC/dt` current term; physical claims require a coupled charge-based comparison, not decorative geometry or arbitrary post-EQ (pp. 146–147). All divisions must follow the intended common path exactly once, preserving generator/percussion/scanner histories during movement (pp. 148–150).
