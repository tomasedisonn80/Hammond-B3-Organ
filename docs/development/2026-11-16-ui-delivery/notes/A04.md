## Engineering implications

The atlas requires revision-specific connected networks before component fidelity
claims. The 1971 122 assembly 036616, vintage 147 and modern 122H are distinct
identities. Full differential volts, per-leg volts, source impedance and load
currents have explicit boundaries; shared parts do not make transfer functions
interchangeable (pp. 253-270). The crossover preserves its floating treble return
and documentary 3.2/5.2 mH and 7.8/12.5 microfarad elements. A generic 800 Hz split
would discard that topology (pp. 271-276).

Each effect has one owner. Driver, horn transit, cabinet, propagation, microphone
and room inclusion must accompany every response asset. Signed pressure and
relative timing survive mixing; independently normalizing angular captures or
adding delay to an already complete kernel destroys the intended behavior.
One physical trajectory per rotor serves every microphone at its own emission
time. UI animation reads snapshots and never advances these clocks (pp. 277-324).

The retarded-time chapter audits two published formula/listing discrepancies:
the circular distance uses the negative cosine term, and linear interpolation
must select sample k at fractional position zero (pp. 302-303). Preserve these
audits when recovering downstream numerical work. Input-time and output-time
angular weighting are different operators during motion, despite identical
frozen-angle responses. Warm history, explicit latency, bounded processing and
complete tails remain necessary (pp. 325-330).
