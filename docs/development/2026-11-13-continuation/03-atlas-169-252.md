## Architectural constraints to preserve now

**Scanner ownership.** Pages 169-174 specify one shared scanner phase and wet
history, two independent routing tablets and six historical selector modes with
no selector OFF position. All tablets off changes routing, not the mechanical
clock. The three node maps on p. 170 are `[1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8,9]`,
`[1,2,3,5,7,9,11,12,13]`, and `[1,2,4,7,10,13,16,18,19]`; paired V/C modes share
a map but differ in common-return topology. These are atlas transcriptions of a
published late-B-3 model, not verified specimen wiring. Ground-relative taps
already contain the common return once (p. 171); adding another generic dry mix
would double-count it.

**Expression and percussion placement.** AO-28 A/B/H entries are distinct;
V3A drives the scanner and V3B the output transformer; expression is between V4
sections and percussion returns after scanner (pp. 193-196, 251). The current
global linear multiplier is useful as a declared surrogate. It is not an AO-28
expression-network reconstruction. Subsequent bus APIs should retain these
boundaries rather than committing to one mixed bus through a generic effect.

**Shared nonlinearity.** When branches meet, process their sum in the declared
shared stage. Per-note saturation omits cross-products (p. 212). Preserve coherent
source reuse before determining peak drive. Host presentation gain and physical
voltage/drive are different quantities (pp. 195, 211). No absolute AO-28 headroom
constant is supplied by this range.

**Physical versus computational state.** All reactive and learned histories have
explicit owners; trial solver states must not partly commit. Preparation, graph
compilation, fitting and profile destruction stay outside audio processing
(pp. 184, 202, 208, 214, 226, 244). Preserving a float buffer through a topology/rate
change is not automatically equivalent to preserving capacitor voltage and
inductor current. For the current M3 scope, exact same-rate state continuity is a
reasonable engineering gate; do not promise unimplemented hot profile migration.

## Evidence gaps and documentary hazards

- The scanner paper discrepancies described on pp. 175-180 require independent
  algebra and all-tap circuit comparison. Eighteen LC sections are not nineteen
  stages. Matrix multiplication order, voltage/current conventions and hertz versus
  radians matter. The atlas's printed toy results are not tests executed here.
- AO-28 V1-V8 includes V3 as a 12BH7 with two sections; inconsistent theory prose
  must not create a ninth socket (pp. 193-198). Tube-family examples and tentative
  datasheets do not establish actual bias or fitted parameters (pp. 205-210).
- The 85 stock records and 124 component/section placements referenced on
  pp. 235-240 neither supply a connected netlist nor count installed packages.
  Their original companion files remain absent at this baseline. Complete active
  nodes, values, variant membership and independent trace are prerequisites for
  circuit export.
- Preserve factory C59 versus the redraw's duplicate C56, the repeated catalog
  callout 6-6-43, unreadable item 47, unresolved R62 and mutually exclusive
  AO-28/no-code alternatives (pp. 236-240). Do not repair ambiguity by picking
  whichever value makes simulation convenient.
- Transformer turns ratio, source/load impedances, actual operating points,
  contact trajectories, calibrated percussion recovery, expression loading,
  scanner observations, noise floor and specimen identity are not supplied as
  acquired measurements (pp. 173, 187-192, 197, 201-204, 209-210, 215-216,
  221-222, 227-228, 240, 249-252).
- Aging is a dated specimen/configuration record, not a generic year slider.
  Uncertainty is metadata rather than automatic audible randomization; no
  between-instrument distribution follows from one specimen (pp. 223-228).
- Atlas “dry organ” includes scanner and AO-28 but excludes Leslie/room (p. 247).
  The repository's current dry-source renderer is a narrower nominal deliverable.
  Preserve that distinction when naming milestone completion.
