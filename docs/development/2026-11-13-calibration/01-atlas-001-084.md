## Whole-plan interpretation from the front matter

Pages 1-12 distinguish the **80 research chapters in ten research waves** from the
**M0-M9 implementation milestones**. Neither count proves product completion.
The target is a particular vintage electromechanical B-3/AO-28 and Leslie 122;
other electrical, cabinet and regional variants retain independent identities
(pp. 2-5). The atlas explicitly states that it acquired **no physical instrument
measurements**, and supplied no completed audio engine, plugin or captured
reference dataset (p. 2). Any present repository implementation must therefore
have its own executed evidence.

The intended order is reference contracts, a testable engine, shared generator,
console behavior, scanner, organ electronics, Leslie electronics, rotary
acoustics, calibration and product qualification (p. 5). Subsystem diagnostics,
provenance, unknowns, reproducible rendering and meaningful tests precede a
polished interface (p. 12). Direct-organ and miked-cabinet outputs have separate
validation boundaries (p. 4). This supports independent M8 infrastructure now,
while physical acceptance depends on actual acquired evidence and upstream
model readiness.

## Concrete requirements for calibration-readiness tooling

1. **Strict stream admission before arithmetic.** Decode only explicitly supported
   formats; verify actual sample format, rate, channel count, frame structure,
   finite samples and bounded sizes against the declaration. Report unsupported
   formats separately instead of assuming that the existing metadata validator's
   format enum means a decoder exists. Header acceptance proves a digital stream,
   not correct physical calibration (pp. 15, 17, 65, 67, 77).
2. **Retain original bytes and all transformation ancestry.** A crop, alignment,
   denoise, gain, resample or channel selection produces an identified derivative
   with input hashes, parameters and tool identity. Never normalize or silently
   realign the raw evidence. A reported comparison must identify the exact bytes
   and sample ranges it actually used (pp. 17, 21, 65, 70-71, 77).
3. **Make corpus independence an explicit gate.** Group synchronized channels,
   related crops, repeated source takes and derived outputs with their parents.
   Detect shared raw identities even when names or directories differ. Freeze
   fit/holdout membership before model tuning; retain declared group rationale
   and session/specimen context. Hash uniqueness alone cannot establish statistical
   independence (pp. 18, 41, 47-48, 65, 71, 77, 83).
4. **Bind comparisons to boundary, level and time conventions.** Preserve source
   IDs, electrical port/load, specimen revision, controls, room/microphone state,
   clock domain and reference time. Distinguish full-scale amplitude from V/FS or
   Pa/FS. Compare common phase only when acquisition supports it. Silent unity
   calibration or equal-header-rate clock equivalence is unacceptable (pp. 41,
   57, 59, 65, 67, 71, 77, 83).
5. **Keep raw and fitted metrics separately visible.** A gain/delay alignment can
   be a declared nuisance-parameter experiment; it must not erase the original
   amplitude, timing or phase error. Frequency error in cents, absolute level,
   waveform residual and complex spectral error answer different questions.
   Report analysis window, bandwidth, signal/noise floor and supported assumptions
   with each metric; no automatic universal fidelity score (pp. 18, 65-66, 72,
   75, 77-78, 83-84).
6. **Use predeclared outcomes and preserve missing evidence.** Freeze the policy
   and numerical bounds before evaluation. Return separate engineering pass/fail
   and evidence-not-established or inconclusive findings. No raw acquisition,
   unqualified synchronization, rank deficiency or unsupported format must become
   a pass because the comparator produced a finite number (pp. 18, 42, 48, 72,
   78, 83-84).
7. **Prove the tools with honestly synthetic cases first.** Analytic signals with
   known offset, gain, phase, polarity, corruption and group leakage can qualify
   software and reveal estimator limits. Keep their synthetic identities through
   reports and examples. They cannot supply the absent hardware dataset, measured
   uncertainty, permission review or calibrated profile (pp. 2, 15-18, 72).
