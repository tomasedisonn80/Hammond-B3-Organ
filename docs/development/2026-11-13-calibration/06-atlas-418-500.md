| --- | --- |
| Audio inspection | Open actual bounded local bytes; verify admitted header, encoding, frame/channel geometry and content; preserve malformed, missing, unsupported and nonfinite outcomes. Test literal byte fixtures independent of the producer. |
| Corpus inventory | Bind manifest/file hashes and stable IDs; resolve ancestry across the corpus; detect conflicting identities, unresolved parents, cycles and duplicate byte streams. Keep paths and digests visible. |

| Fixed comparison | Bind exact candidate/reference channels, boundaries, polarity, units, clocks, initial state, windows, bandwidth and permitted transforms before calculation. Retain excluded coverage and invalid denominators. |
| Frozen decision/report | Identify policy, metric recipe, threshold origin, source/profile/data versions and reviewer; retain pass, fail, blocked/not-run and inconclusive. Replaying a report must verify its actual inputs and recompute its claims. |

The v1 `digital` object describes the raw acquisition stream. Derived artifacts may legitimately change rate, format or channel count; they need their own explicit transformation/analysis metadata rather than automatic comparison with every raw header.
Format admission is not physical calibration. A dependency-free inspector must declare its supported subset and report unsupported encodings honestly without changing v1's legal declaration set.
Byte equality proves shared content but cannot establish independent takes; a renamed path or separate valid local DAG cannot defeat cross-corpus grouping (pp. 452, 455; acquisition protocol section 7).
Shared independently obtained calibration references may support multiple groups with correlated uncertainty; using them to optimize the sound model changes their role and requires a renewed split audit.
No new acquisition, remote upload, runtime dependency or Engine change belongs to this scope. Scanner, rotary and standalone code retain their separately assigned owners.

## Comparison rules to freeze before implementation grading

1. Reject incompatible physical comparisons: G-G, pickup, loaded terminal, differential electrical and microphone boundaries are distinct; table indexing cannot convert one into another (pp. 423, 459, 489).
2. Keep raw evidence immutable. Alignment, gain normalization, resampling, windowing and delay removal create derived assets with parents, recipes and versions; display alignment must not silently remove tested latency (p. 423).
3. Name the actual nuisance parameters permitted by each study. Unconstrained gain, detuning, cabinet EQ or delay fitting can hide upstream errors and is not metrology (pp. 449, 455, 490).
4. Freeze metric units, bandwidth, windows, normalization, channel pairing, uncertainty treatment and thresholds before final held-out examination. The atlas supplies no universal numerical fidelity tolerance (p. 484).
5. Preserve insufficient-resolution cases as inconclusive. Unknown uncertainty is not zero; residual size and uncertainty describe different things (pp. 423, 462, 484).
6. Keep objective, listening and playable-response claims separate. Nonsignificance is not equivalence, and a listening preference cannot identify wrong wiring (pp. 449, 484).
7. Preserve failed and superseded studies. A changed threshold or a final set used to select a revision creates a new study; old results cannot be relabeled pass (pp. 481, 486).
