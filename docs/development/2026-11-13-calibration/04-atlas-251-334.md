## Requirements for the independent offline tooling

1. **Preserve evidence classes.** Reports must distinguish documentary inputs, synthetic numerical checks,
   calibrated hardware comparison and listening evidence. Missing captures cannot produce a hardware pass
   (pp. 252, 258, 264, 270, 306, 312, 318, 324, 330).
2. **Inspect bytes without inventing calibration.** Verify actual audio format, channel count, sample rate,
   length, finite samples and integrity under explicit bounds, while keeping physical clock and scale
   separate. Header agreement cannot establish shared-clock acquisition or volts/pascals (pp. 257, 275, 305, 317).
3. **Retain identity and applicability.** Bind specimen, assembly/revision, adapter, source/load, driver,
   panel/diffuser state, microphone scene and stimulus to immutable files. Changes invalidate only the
   affected comparison domain; a generic cabinet name is inadequate (pp. 253, 259, 265, 295, 300, 307).
4. **Keep grouping stronger than filenames.** Audit raw/derived ancestry and shared acquisition sessions;
   reserve complete poses, angles, repetitions, level/registration combinations and transitions before fitting.
   Crops, rechannels and renamed copies cannot become independent evidence. The duplicate-byte/crop audit
   is this session's proposed enforcement of those source requirements, not an atlas-supplied algorithm
   (pp. 251, 263, 269, 281, 287, 300, 317, 329).
5. **Freeze transformations.** Declare windows, input scale, filtering, resampling, latency and permitted
   nuisance alignment before evaluation. Keep raw and transformed comparisons separately reproducible;
   forbid silent per-angle/per-channel gain or peak alignment and motion time warping (pp. 263, 280,
   293, 298, 305, 309, 317, 333).
6. **Preserve signs and shared time.** Analysis must retain differential/per-leg distinction, channel maps,
   absolute timing, removed delays and their single reapplication. Compare coherent signed outputs;
   magnitude-only error misses polarity, arrival and interference mistakes (pp. 255, 272, 300, 308, 333).
7. **Use declared metrics and reliability.** Begin with bounded waveform/level checks and explicit windows;
   add complex response, arrivals, sidebands, cross-spectrum and tails only with their applicable contract.
   Report excluded null/noise bins and insufficient reliable coverage rather than claiming zero phase error
   or collapsing uncertainty into one similarity percentage (pp. 287-288, 305, 311-312, 318, 330).
8. **Make acceptance replayable.** Retain source/data/profile hashes, event scripts, code/tool versions,
   analysis settings, predeclared tolerances, failure reasons and scope. Independently constructed examples
   should expose gain/sign/delay mistakes, common-channel timing loss, incomplete tails and disguised split
   leakage; compare supported rates with declared reconstruction rather than byte identity (pp. 252, 270, 330, 333).

These are companion-contract requirements. Existing capture v1 declaration and planned/recorded semantics
must remain intact; neither an inspected file nor a schema-valid manifest proves physical authenticity.
