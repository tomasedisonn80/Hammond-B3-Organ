## Consequences for the independent calibration-readiness track

The proposed M8 tooling is justified without taking ownership of the ongoing scanner, rotary or standalone
implementations. Its first useful output is an auditable analysis pipeline that can admit future evidence while
refusing to convert synthetic fixtures into measurements.

### 1. Admission must preserve the measurement boundary

At minimum retain immutable raw artifact identity, actual digital format/rate/ channel count, specimen and
configuration identity, acquisition session, channel-reference polarity, source/load conditions, control/state
history, physical clock status and amplitude calibration. Unknown calibration cannot silently become unity gain or
nominal clock. A header/finite-sample check proves signal-container properties; it cannot prove specimen identity,
permissions, correct loading, calibration or an original recording (pp. 187, 197, 199, 203, 215, 221, 247).

Recorded metadata should continue to distinguish synthetic and hardware records. Synthetic WAVs can provide exact
independent parser, metric and leakage fixtures. They cannot satisfy the physical-recording requirements of pp. 192,
204, 216, 222, 228, 240 or 246. Preserve planned records without requiring nonexistent artifact paths, and keep
unsupported encodings or insufficient observations explicitly blocked or inconclusive rather than guessed.

### 2. Corpus splits require more than file-level random selection

Group complete takes, sessions, related windows and derived artifacts together. The intended scientific claim
determines stronger grouping: complete modes or frequencies for scanner generalization (p. 185), bias traces/screen
slices for device fitting (p. 209), loads/controls for transformer and amplifier behavior (p. 203), and whole
specimens/service sessions for population claims (p. 228). Retain a separate model-selection purpose rather than
repeatedly inspecting the final holdout (pp. 191, 243, 245).

Audit exact raw-content duplicates and inherited lineage across manifests; renaming or cropping an existing take
must not create independent holdout evidence. Metadata grouping and digest checks can expose declared or byte-exact
leakage but cannot establish the independence of undeclared physical takes. Freeze the split and its reasons before
tuning and retain later amendments as new identified versions.

### 3. Start with transparent metrics and declared alignment

Useful initial measures are finite-sample/count checks, DC, peak, RMS, clipping, crest factor, fixed-alignment
waveform error and boundary-compatible complex tone response. Keep samples or values before any normalization. Known
transport delay alignment must be distinguished from fitted alignment or physical group delay; per-tap shifts,
independently normalized output legs and arbitrary time-warping can conceal errors (pp. 179, 191, 203, 233, 243,
248).

For later stationary response estimation, p. 188 specifies synchronized cross-spectral transfer and coherence. Phase
and group delay require a declared excitation/magnitude floor and cannot be graded at nulls. The rotating scanner
requires time-varying/sideband analysis, not one ordinary LTI response. Retain one shared rotor trajectory; distinct
fitted trajectories per test frequency are invalid compensation (pp. 188-190).

### 4. Freeze acceptance before observing the final outcome

Store numerical precision budgets separately from physical uncertainty and perceptual criteria. Each result should
retain input identities, model/tool versions, units, timing convention, analyzed windows, excluded-bin reasons,
calibration status and its own pass/fail/not-run/blocked or inconclusive state. Report failures by route, mode,
frequency, level and condition so an aggregate score cannot mask them (pp. 174, 186, 192, 204, 210, 234, 250).

A comparison with absent/unknown physical calibration may still produce useful digital statistics. Its report must
keep the unclosed physical gate. Fit interpretable upstream boundaries before downstream compensation; no fitted EQ,
gain or residual should silently conceal wrong topology or double-count an already captured response (pp. 191, 204,
211, 224, 242-246).
