## Physical admission and uncertainty

Schema-valid, byte-verified, header-consistent hardware declarations can still be physically
unusable (pp. 355-356, 360). Catalogue admission and physical-fit admission need separate
outputs. Synthetic input always retains its synthetic evidence class.

- Require an identified specimen/revision/configuration and measurement boundary, with
  load, polarity, controls, warmup, chain response, calibration and permissions (pp. 343-354).
- Preserve digital header rate separately from actual sample-clock calibration and clock
  uncertainty. Synchronization does not establish absolute clock accuracy (pp. 351, 356, 364).
- V/FS or Pa/FS scale does not establish frequency/phase response, latency or impedance;
  retain the chain-calibration reference and channel skew separately (pp. 351, 356).
- Preserve null plus explanation for unknown values. A standard uncertainty and an interval
  are different; both use the quantity's unit, with coherent bounds where applicable (p. 356).
- Repetition estimates only part of uncertainty. Shared gain, clock and geometry errors
  correlate samples/channels/bins; retain those dependencies (pp. 347, 354, 363-364).
- When uncertainty cannot resolve the frozen error budget, report inconclusive and improve
  acquisition. Increasing uncertainty must never make certification easier (p. 347).
- Quarantine failed, clipped, dropped, ambiguous or failed-calibration intervals with reasons;
  preserve their records and excluded coverage (pp. 351, 354). A floating file cannot undo ADC clipping.
- The factory G-G service procedure, including its historical adjustment criterion, provides
  neither per-source oscillator amplitudes nor emulation tolerances (pp. 350, 355, 361, 368).

## Frozen analysis, transforms and decision policy

Before grading candidates, freeze capture IDs, analysis windows, units, feature definitions,
weights, permitted corrections, coverage, tolerances and aggregation (pp. 347, 363-366, 372).
Keep the uncorrected comparison beside the permitted corrected comparison (p. 364).

| Evidence-supported correction | Compensation that would hide disagreement |
| --- | --- |
| One calibrated channel-group time offset | Per-note/window warping or deleting physical delay |
| Clock correction with retained uncertainty | Source detuning to absorb recorder drift |
| Shared calibrated gain nuisance | Independent gain for each registration or segment |
| Constrained shared initial generator/rotor state | Independent partial/note/revolution phase resets |
| Measured microphone response correction | Unreported residual equalization |

The table follows p. 364; p. 375 further requires frozen listening gains, stereo balance,
peak checks and every resampling/routing/fade recipe. Absolute-level validation and a
restricted listening-level comparison remain separate products.

Use boundary-specific metrics: complex transfer and calibrated level, arrival time, event
and envelope timing, short/long spectra, motion trajectories, modulation sidebands,
stereo coherence and mono sum (pp. 347, 363, 371). Magnitude alone cannot qualify phase.
The proposed complex metric is `sqrt(sum(w*abs(Hm-Hr)^2)/sum(w*abs(Hr)^2))`, with
predeclared nonnegative reliability weights (p. 347). Zero-reference energy needs an explicit
undefined/inconclusive policy; this note proposes that safeguard, not an atlas numeric rule.
Preserve low-SNR/null exclusions, measured noise floors and per-condition coverage. Treating
correlated summaries as independent observations or heuristic weights as likelihood is invalid
(pp. 347, 363). Preserve data error separately from prior/regularization cost (pp. 363, 365).
Stage-wise upstream fitting needs its own observed ports and numerical refinement; one optimizer
minimum or a low output residual cannot establish identifiable internal parameters (pp. 361-366).
