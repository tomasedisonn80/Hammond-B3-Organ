## Concrete requirements for the independent calibration tool track

These are implementation recommendations inferred from the reviewed requirements;
they do not modify the already owned scanner, rotary or standalone contracts.

### Admission and immutable evidence

An admitted capture should bind the manifest's format, sample rate, channels and
sample count to inspected bytes. A digest-only check cannot detect a declaration
that describes the wrong audio representation. Keep supported-format limits
explicit and reject malformed or unsupported encodings rather than reinterpret
them. Retain original asset hashes and a separately identified recipe for crop,
alignment, unit conversion or any normalized derivative (pp. 91, 94-96, 119).

Carry source boundary, polarity, calibration unit, fixed gain, load and clock
meaning into analysis reports. Digital full-scale metrics can be computed before
physical calibration, but they must retain that unit and must not silently become
volts or pascals. The G-G, raw-pickup, loaded-terminal and acoustic boundaries are
different experiments (pp. 92-93, 113, 143, 149, 158).

### Corpus splits and retained failure evidence

Split by whole related acquisitions before fitting: sessions and wheel groups
(p. 89), gestures/takes/keys (p. 119), pedals/mixtures (p. 143), and complete
revolutions/frequencies (p. 167). A derivative of a fitting take must not reappear
as supposedly independent held-out evidence. A corpus audit should detect repeated
raw identities and ancestry crossing declared roles, while reporting that distinct
hashes alone do not prove physical independence. Keep failed, incomplete-release
and low-signal cases visible instead of silently dropping them (pp. 131, 149).

### Metrics, alignment and acceptance

Preserve absolute acquisition level and phase. Report the alignment transform and
its estimation basis separately; never independently normalize takes to improve
percussion or expression scores. Compare only compatible boundaries and declared
time/phase bases; unsynchronized tonewheel takes do not support a raw waveform
null (pp. 95, 137, 144, 149).

Provide distinct outputs for peak/RMS/DC, noise/clipping, source-frequency error,
complex carrier amplitude/phase, transient/envelope error and normalized residual.
The initial scope can implement a clearly bounded subset. A near-zero reference
must make relative error unavailable or inconclusive under an explicit floor,
not fabricate zero error or an infinite JSON number. Preserve excluded bins,
windows, frequency, sample rate, coefficients and reasons (pp. 113, 120, 137-138,
149). Predeclare thresholds and distinguish numerical project targets from
repeatability-supported physical gates (pp. 120, 138, 150, 161-162).

### Useful synthetic acceptance cases

- Exact digital identity; known signed gain, DC, fixed delay and channel swap.
- Pure sinusoidal AM with known complex sidebands, separately from phase
  modulation and additive hum (pp. 86, 90).
- Two coherent branches and intentionally incoherent phases, proving amplitude
  summation and preventing power-sum expectations (p. 111).
- Known contact-edge phase cases and a genuinely zero-reference window, with
  explicit latency/gain conventions (pp. 116, 120).
- Shared raw ancestry across fit/held-out groups, duplicate content under new
  names, and a preserved rejected/inconclusive measurement record.

Synthetic tests establish these software operations only. They cannot provide
missing specimen identity, electrical loading, independent clocks, absolute gain,
mechanical gestures, covariance, hardware repeatability or physical fidelity.
